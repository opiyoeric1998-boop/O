About me

From the literature classroom to the codebase, without leaving either.

I’m an English & Literature teacher who builds software. Moving into Computer Science isn’t a change of direction; it’s a widening of it.

Good teaching and good software share the same rule: start with the person who has to use it.

My story

Educator first. Builder by necessity, then by choice.

My foundation is in English and Literature. I earned my Bachelor’s degree from Kaimosi Friends University, and in the classroom I learned the skills I still lean on most: explaining complex ideas simply, listening closely, and structuring information so people can actually use it.

Working inside a school showed me how much time is lost to paperwork, scattered records and manual processes. Instead of waiting for someone else to fix it, I started building. One website became a full school management system; that experience led to point of sale and inventory systems for retail businesses, each one planned, built, deployed and handed over to the people who use it daily.

Today I’m formalising that practice with a Bachelor’s degree in Computer Science at the University of the People. I’m not leaving education behind; I’m expanding what I can offer it. The goal is to stand confidently in both worlds: someone who understands learners and institutions, and who can build the technology that serves them.

What I bring

A teacher’s patience for requirements and training, a writer’s care for clarity, and a developer’s focus on systems that are secure, reliable and simple to use.
